Residential & Commercial Concrete Installation

Concrete Sidewalks & Walkways

Walkways create convenient routes around homes and businesses. Proper grading, joint placement, and surface texture support function and accessibility. Decorative options include stamped and stained finishes.

Concrete Shed Pads

A stable, level pad can support an appropriate storage shed or outdoor structure. Dimensions, thickness, and reinforcement must match expected loads and manufacturer requirements.

Concrete Driveway Aprons

Aprons create transitions between driveways and adjacent surfaces. Elevation, drainage, and any public right-of-way requirements should be considered. Related to full driveway installation.

Concrete Parking Pads

Parking pads provide designated areas for cars, trailers, or other equipment. The design should reflect expected weight and traffic.

Small Commercial Concrete Projects

Concrete can be used for entry areas, pathways, and equipment pads, with requirements dependent on access, drainage, and traffic.

Why Proper Concrete Installation Matters

Site Preparation

A stable, properly prepared base reduces the risk of unwanted settlement.

Drainage & Grading

Good grading helps move water away from buildings and usable surfaces.

Concrete Thickness & Reinforcement

Specifications should reflect the intended use and expected loads.

Finishing & Control Joints

Texture helps determine traction, while joints help manage shrinkage cracking.

Concrete Replacement & Surface Improvements

Cracked, settled, or deteriorating surfaces may need replacement. A new installation offers opportunities to correct drainage, adjust dimensions, and upgrade finishes. Outdoor living spaces may also start with a concrete patio.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does concrete installation cost?

Costs vary by dimensions, thickness, excavation, reinforcement, access, and finish. A site visit produces a written estimate for your project.

How thick should a concrete pad be?

Many standard residential pads are about four inches thick, but heavier loads and certain structures need different specifications. Tell us what will sit on the pad.

Can concrete be poured during winter?

Yes, in some conditions, with proper cold-weather procedures and curing protection. Scheduling depends on temperature and the protection plan.

How long does concrete take to cure?

Concrete often reaches its specified design strength at about 28 days; use timing depends on the project and conditions. Follow the installer directions before loading the slab.

Can concrete be installed over old concrete?

Some overlays are possible on stable existing slabs. Failed or moving concrete commonly needs removal.

Do I need a permit for concrete installation?

Requirements vary by municipality and project type, particularly near public sidewalks or roadways. We can discuss typical local expectations when we review the job.
