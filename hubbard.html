Concrete Contractors in Hubbard, Ohio

Hubbard is east of Youngstown, toward the Pennsylvania line. Residential drives and patios here sit on the clay soils typical of this side of the valley. Clay holds water, and a slab poured on wet, unprepared ground will move. The work is ordinary and specific: compact a base, slope the surface, and joint the concrete so winter has a place to crack it straight.

What changes on a Hubbard lot

Downspouts matter in Hubbard as much as the concrete. A patio at the back door will stay wet if a spout dumps beside it. We look at that, and at whether you want one plan for a broom-finish drive and a stamped walk, before we schedule a truck.

Questions

Hubbard concrete questions

Do you serve Hubbard, toward the Pennsylvania line?

Yes. Hubbard is on our service area list with Youngstown and the nearby townships. If your address is past Hubbard and you are unsure, call and we will tell you whether we can schedule it.

How should a Hubbard driveway be sloped on a clay lot?

Away from the garage and off the slab, into a yard or the street that can take the water. Clay will not soak up a puddle the way sandy ground might. If the yard has nowhere for that water to go, we talk about grade before we pour.

Can you stamp a front walk and broom-finish the drive in one plan?

Yes. They can be poured together or staged, with a joint between the decorative walk and the drive. The stamp has to happen while that section is fresh. We will not try to stamp the drive later because the walk turned out well.

What should I do about downspouts before a patio pour?

Point them somewhere other than the new slab or the foundation. Extensions that dump at the edge of the patio will stain it and keep the base wet. We will flag a spout that is going to fight the pour.
