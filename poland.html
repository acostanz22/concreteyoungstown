Concrete Contractors in Poland, Ohio

Poland is southeast of Youngstown. Village streets can be tight for a concrete truck, and township properties outside the village often have more grade and a longer approach. The work is driveways, private walks, and patios that deal with freeze-thaw and with water moving across a sloped yard.

What changes on a Poland lot

We plan truck access before pour day. A village drive with limited swing room may be placed by buggy from the street. On a sloped township lot, the first question is where the water goes after it leaves the slab, not which broom or stamp you like.

Questions

Poland concrete questions

Can a concrete truck reach a Poland village driveway?

Sometimes it can, and sometimes we stage on the street and move the concrete in. We check the width, the overhead lines, and the parked cars before we promise a truck in the drive. Access is written into the estimate.

Do you replace sidewalk squares that have heaved?

Yes, on private walks, and on the portion of a front walk that is yours. One lifted square can be cut out and repoured if its neighbors are stable. If the whole run has moved, one square will not level the path.

What finish holds up on a Poland driveway that sees road salt?

A broom finish on air-entrained concrete, with joints and a real base, is the durable everyday choice. Salt will still mark a surface over the years. A decorative stamp is optional, and it needs a texture you can shovel. We will not sell a sealer as protection from winter.

Can you add a patio without blocking a basement window?

Yes. We keep the slab and the grade below an egress opening and out of the window well. If a patio design would cover that well, we change the layout. A bigger slab is not worth a blocked exit.
